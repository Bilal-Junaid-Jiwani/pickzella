Every Pickzella guide follows the same research process. This page explains exactly what that process is, and just as importantly, what it is not.

## What we do before recommending anything

**We define the use case first.** A "best" list is meaningless without knowing who it is for. Each guide states its audience up front: beginners, small apartments, tight budgets, desk workers. If a product is great but wrong for the stated use case, it does not make the list.

**We build a shortlist from the whole market.** We start broad, looking at what is actually available and popular on Amazon.com and from established retailers, then narrow down to the contenders worth a closer look. Obscure listings with a handful of reviews do not make the cut, and neither do products with suspicious review patterns.

**We compare the specifications that matter.** Dimensions, materials, battery capacity, charging speeds, warranties, return windows. We lay these out side by side because the numbers that affect daily ownership are often different from the numbers on the box. Marketing leads with the biggest number; we look for the number you will actually feel.

**We read buyer reviews in bulk.** For each contender, we go through large numbers of verified buyer reviews and look for patterns: the same praise repeated across dozens of buyers is signal, and so is the same complaint. A single angry review means little. Forty buyers describing the same hinge failure means a lot. This is the most time-consuming part of the process, and the part most "best of" lists skip.

**We weigh value over hype.** The winner is rarely the most expensive option and rarely the cheapest. It is the one that does the job well for a fair price, with failure modes we can live with. When a budget pick genuinely holds up, we say so. When spending more buys real durability, we say that too.

**We list the cons, even on products we link to.** Every pick card includes drawbacks. If a product has a real weakness and we know about it, hiding it would cost you more than it earns us.

## What we do not do

We do not run a testing lab. We say "research" and mean it: spec comparison, review analysis, and category expertise, not bench tests or measurement rigs. Any site that claims lab testing should be able to show you the lab. We would rather be honest about desk research than invent a testing program.

We do not take paid placements. Brands cannot pay to appear in our guides or to move up a ranking. Our revenue comes from affiliate links, which you can read about in our [affiliate disclosure](/disclosure/).

Product cards marked as placeholders are exactly that: placeholder entries shown while final picks are being confirmed. The buying advice in each guide is written to stand on its own, with or without the cards.

## How guides stay current

Products get discontinued, prices shift, and new models arrive. We revisit guides to check that picks are still available and still good value, and we update the last-updated date on the page when we do. If a former pick has been replaced by something better, we say so rather than quietly swapping it.

If you spot an error in a guide, or a product we should look at, the fastest way to reach us is the [contact page](/contact/). Corrections go in, credited or not, your choice.
