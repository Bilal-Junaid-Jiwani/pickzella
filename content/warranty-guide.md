## Read this first

Two honest caveats. First, our buying guides describe picks by type, not brand name, because we compare specifications and buyer feedback rather than logos. So this page does something different: it gives verified warranty and return snapshots for well-known brands and programs in the categories we cover, so you know what "good coverage" looks like wherever you buy.

Second: **policies change.** Everything below is as listed on brand and retailer sites in October 2026. Always verify the current terms on the brand's own site before buying.

## Verified snapshots (October 2026)

| Brand / program | Standard warranty | Return window | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple Certified Refurbished | 1-year limited warranty | 14 days | New battery fitted in refurbished iOS devices; AppleCare+ can be purchased, extending coverage |
| Amazon Renewed | Renewed Guarantee, 90-day returns | 90 days from receipt | Renewed Premium and Renewed Automotive extend to 365 days; battery must test at 80%+ of original capacity (90%+ for Premium) |
| Cuisinart (nonstick cookware) | Limited lifetime warranty | Varies by retailer | Covers defects in material and workmanship under normal home use; the nonstick coating wears with use, which is normal, not a defect |
| Ninja (air fryers) | 1-year limited warranty | Varies by retailer (often 30 days) | Spare parts like containers are sold separately, so a cracked basket does not retire the whole appliance |

Sources: Apple's Certified Refurbished terms and Amazon's Renewed Guarantee page, plus warranty documentation and retailer listings for Cuisinart and SharkNinja, as published in 2026.

## What to check for every category we cover

**Earbuds and audio.** Look for a 1-year manufacturer warranty minimum and ask who handles the claim: the brand or the marketplace seller. Our [wireless earbuds guide](/posts/best-wireless-earbuds-under-50-2026/) explains why fit matters more than brand here; a short warranty on a $35 pair is acceptable, but the return window is what actually protects you.

**Power banks and batteries.** The warranty matters less than the safety certifications. Check for UL or equivalent marks and a clear battery-capacity policy. See our [power banks guide](/posts/best-power-banks-travel-2026/).

**Air fryers and kitchen appliances.** A 1-year warranty is the industry norm, as the Ninja snapshot shows. The real differentiator is spare parts: brands that sell replacement baskets and crisper plates separately are telling you the appliance is meant to last.

**Cookware.** Lifetime and limited-lifetime warranties are common on hard-anodized and stainless sets, but they cover defects, not wear. Nonstick coating is a wear item by nature. Our [cookware guide](/posts/best-nonstick-cookware-sets-2026/) covers the ceramic and PTFE options honestly.

**Fitness gear.** Dumbbells, rollers, ropes, and bands rarely carry meaningful warranties, and that is fine: there is almost nothing to break. What matters is the retailer's return window for the first two weeks, when a sizing mistake shows up. See our [dumbbells guide](/posts/best-adjustable-dumbbells-small-spaces-2026/) and [resistance bands guide](/posts/best-resistance-bands-home-workouts-2026/).

**Robot vacuums.** These are the highest-risk category on this page: complex, expensive, and the ones most likely to need service. Prioritize a 1-year warranty and check whether the brand has a US service network before buying. Our [robot vacuum guide](/posts/best-robot-vacuums-pet-hair-2026/) has the details.

**Smart home.** Matter certification matters more than the warranty card. A plug that works across Alexa, Google, and Apple Home will outlive any single ecosystem. See our [smart plugs guide](/posts/best-smart-plugs-beginners-2026/).

## Return windows: the part people forget

The warranty covers defects in month eight. The return window covers "this is not what I expected" in week two, and week two is when most buyer regret happens. When comparing two options, the longer return window is often worth more than the longer warranty. Thirty days is standard at most US retailers; some offer 60 or 90, and membership programs sometimes extend it further.

## Keep your receipt, register the product

Two free moves that make warranties work: keep the receipt (a photo in your phone is fine), and fill out the product registration card or online form when the brand offers one. Registration is also how companies reach you if a product is ever recalled, which is exactly what our [recalls page](/recalls/) is about.
