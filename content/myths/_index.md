## Shopping advice that sounds true but is not

Product marketing runs on half-truths that feel like physics. Below are five of the most persistent ones we run into while researching our [buying guides](/), each with a one-line verdict and the honest explanation. No lab coats were harmed: this is general consumer knowledge, not test data.

## Myth 1: More watts means better sound {#watts}

**Verdict: No.**

Wattage measures power draw, not sound quality. A well-designed 20-watt speaker beats a sloppy 100-watt speaker the way a good cook with a small stove beats a bad cook with a commercial range. What actually determines how a speaker sounds is the driver design, the enclosure, and the tuning. Ignore the wattage number; read about the bass response and whether owners mention distortion at high volume.

## Myth 2: You need to spend $200+ for good earbuds {#earbuds-price}

**Verdict: No.**

This one is our most-researched bust. Our [wireless earbuds under $50](/posts/best-wireless-earbuds-under-50-2026/) guide exists because the budget floor has risen dramatically: Bluetooth 5.3, 30-hour cases, and genuinely pleasant tuning are now standard at $35. What the extra $150 buys you is a better microphone, stronger noise cancellation, and a nicer app. If your use case is music and podcasts, the expensive pair is a luxury, not a necessity.

## Myth 3: Higher thread count means better sheets {#thread-count}

**Verdict: Not exactly.**

Thread count counts threads per square inch, and manufacturers can inflate it with thinner, lower-quality threads or creative counting (ply-twisted yarns counted multiple times). A 400-thread-count sheet made from long-staple cotton feels better than an 800-thread-count sheet made from short, coarse fibers. Fiber quality and weave matter more than the number. Treat thread counts above 600 with suspicion, not admiration.

## Myth 4: Nonstick cookware is unsafe {#nonstick-safety}

**Verdict: It depends.**

The old worry was about PFOA, a chemical once used in making PTFE coatings. PFOA has been phased out of cookware manufacturing for years, and our [nonstick cookware guide](/posts/best-nonstick-cookware-sets-2026/) covers ceramic PFAS-free options for anyone who wants to skip the question entirely. The remaining rule is practical, not chemical: do not overheat an empty nonstick pan on high, use wooden or silicone utensils, and replace pans when the coating is badly scratched. Used normally, modern nonstick is not the hazard the old headlines describe.

## Myth 5: More megapixels means a better camera {#megapixels}

**Verdict: Not quite.**

Megapixels measure resolution, which only matters when you crop or print large. Image quality mostly comes from the sensor size, the lens, and the processing. A 12-megapixel photo from a large sensor with a good lens looks better than a 48-megapixel photo from a tiny sensor with aggressive sharpening. This is why phone cameras with "fewer" megapixels routinely out-shoot cheap cameras with more. Judge cameras by sensor size and real sample photos, not the megapixel headline.
