Refresh rate is the number of times per second a display redraws its image, measured in hertz (Hz). A 60Hz screen redraws 60 times per second; a 120Hz screen redraws 120 times. The higher the number, the smoother motion looks.

You notice it most in scrolling, window animations, and games. Text gliding up a 120Hz phone screen stays readable in a way it does not at 60Hz, and mouse movement on a high-refresh monitor feels more precise. It is one of those specs that sounds abstract until you use it, after which going back feels sluggish.

The battery question comes up a lot. Early 120Hz phones paid for smoothness with noticeably worse battery life, but modern displays use adaptive refresh rates: they ramp up to 120Hz when you are scrolling and drop to 10Hz or lower when the screen is static. The cost is now small enough that most people should leave the high refresh rate on.

Where it does not matter: anything that is not a screen. Refresh rate has no bearing on audio gear, lighting, or kitchen appliances, despite occasionally appearing in marketing copy. For TVs, note that the panel's native refresh rate matters more than motion-smoothing features, which interpolate frames and create the "soap opera effect" many viewers dislike.
