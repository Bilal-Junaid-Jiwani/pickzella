USB-C PD (Power Delivery) is the charging standard that lets a USB-C charger and device negotiate exactly how much power to use, from a trickle for earbuds up to 240W for large laptops on the latest PD 3.1 spec. It is the reason one compact GaN charger can now replace the pile of device-specific bricks most of us used to carry.

The negotiation is the key part. When you plug in, the two sides agree on a voltage and current both can handle, so a 65W charger safely charges a 5W earbud case without any risk of overloading it. Fast charging is simply the device asking for, and receiving, more watts.

To actually get those speeds, the whole chain has to support PD: the charger, the cable, and the device. Cables matter more than people expect. A cable rated for 60W will cap a 100W charge, and unmarked bargain cables are the most common reason fast charging silently fails. The fix is a cable from a reputable brand with its wattage printed on it.

PD is also what makes modern power banks useful for laptops: a bank advertising 65W PD output can genuinely charge a laptop, not just a phone. When comparing banks or chargers, the PD wattage is the number that tells you what it can actually power.
