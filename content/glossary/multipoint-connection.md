Multipoint is a Bluetooth feature that lets one device stay connected to two source devices at once and switch between them on its own. The classic use case is earbuds paired to both your phone and your laptop: a video plays on the laptop, a call comes in on the phone, and the earbuds switch to the call automatically, then switch back when you hang up.

Without multipoint, you either re-pair manually or rely on basic auto-switching that many devices handle clumsily. Multipoint removes that friction entirely, which is why it has become one of the most genuinely useful features on modern earbuds.

There are two flavors. Simple multipoint keeps both connections live but plays audio from one at a time. Advanced multipoint can mix or intelligently prioritize, but in practice most earbuds do the simple version, and that is all most people need.

One caveat: the feature lives in the earbuds, not the phone, so both connections depend on the earbuds' implementation. If auto-switching misbehaves, check the companion app for a multipoint toggle and make sure the firmware is current.
