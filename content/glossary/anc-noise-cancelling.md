Active noise cancelling (ANC) uses tiny microphones to listen to the noise around you, then plays an inverted copy of that sound wave to cancel it out before it reaches your ears. The result is a noticeably quieter world, especially on planes, trains, and near humming appliances.

ANC is at its best against steady, low-frequency noise: jet engines, bus rumble, air conditioners, office HVAC. It struggles with sudden or irregular sounds like conversations, clattering dishes, or a dog barking, because the system cannot predict and invert them fast enough. That is why a good physical seal matters too: the ear tips block the highs while the electronics erase the lows.

Most ANC earbuds and headphones offer a transparency mode as well, which does the reverse and pipes outside sound in, useful for hearing announcements or staying aware while walking. Wind remains the weak point of both modes: gusts hitting the microphones create a rushing sound that ANC cannot fully remove.

When shopping, ignore marketing superlatives and check two things instead: whether reviewers describe the ANC as effective in real commutes, and how much battery life drops with ANC on. A strong physical seal often matters more than the ANC brand name on the box.
