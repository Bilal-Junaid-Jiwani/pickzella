IPX5 means a device is protected against sustained low-pressure water jets sprayed from any direction. In everyday terms, it shrugs off rain, sweat, and splashes without trouble.

The "5" is the liquid-protection digit of the IP (Ingress Protection) code, and the "X" means the device was not rated for dust or solid-particle protection. That is common: many earbuds and speakers carry an IPX rating rather than a full IP rating.

What IPX5 does not cover is submersion. The test behind the rating involves spraying the device with water jets for several minutes, not dunking it. If you want something that survives a drop in the pool or a swim, look for IPX7 (survives 30 minutes under 1 meter of water) or IPX8 (deeper or longer, per the manufacturer).

For most buyers, IPX5 is the sweet spot for workout and outdoor gear. Rain and sweat are handled, and you are not paying for submersion protection you will never use. Just remember that water resistance is not permanent: seals wear over time, and soaps, salt water, and drops all degrade it faster.
