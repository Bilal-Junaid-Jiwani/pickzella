## Why this page exists

Every buying guide on this site ends at the purchase. This page picks up where they stop. Products get recalled every week in the US: chargers with faulty batteries, furniture with tip-over risks, kitchen appliances with wiring faults. Checking takes two minutes, and it is the single highest-value safety habit a shopper can have.

One important note on scope: this page tells you how to check recalls. It does not track specific recalls, and we never claim any particular product has been recalled. Recall status changes constantly; only the official sources below are authoritative.

## How to check a product for recalls

**Search the CPSC recall database.** The US Consumer Product Safety Commission publishes every recall at [cpsc.gov/recalls](https://www.cpsc.gov/recalls). Search by product name, brand, or category. This is the first place to check before buying anything secondhand, refurbished, or from an unfamiliar seller.

**Check complaints on SaferProducts.gov.** The CPSC's public database at [SaferProducts.gov](https://www.saferproducts.gov) collects consumer reports of incidents and injuries. A product does not need an official recall to appear here, so it is useful for spotting patterns: the same failure reported by a dozen owners is signal.

**Check recalls.gov for everything else.** The CPSC covers most consumer products, but not all of them. Vehicles and car seats fall under the Department of Transportation (NHTSA), and food, drugs, and cosmetics fall under the FDA. [Recalls.gov](https://www.recalls.gov) links the recall databases across federal agencies, so one search covers the gaps.

**Call if you prefer.** The CPSC hotline is 800-638-2772 (TTY 800-638-8270). Real humans, real answers.

## Make recalls come to you

The easiest version of this habit is the one you set up once:

- **Email alerts:** subscribe to CPSC recall alerts at cpsc.gov so new recalls land in your inbox.
- **Register your products.** Fill out the registration card or online form that comes with appliances, baby gear, and electronics. Registration is how companies reach you directly if your specific product is recalled.
- **Photograph the model number.** When you buy an appliance or piece of fitness equipment, snap a photo of the model and serial number plate and keep it in your phone. Recall notices identify products by model number, not by what they look like.

## What to do if something you own is recalled

Stop using it immediately. Then follow the remedy in the recall notice: it will be a repair, a replacement, or a refund, and the notice tells you exactly how to claim it. Do not sell it, donate it, or list it secondhand: under federal law, it is illegal to sell a product that is under a CPSC recall.

## Before you buy used or refurbished

This is where recall checks matter most. A used air fryer or a refurbished space heater can be a great deal, and it can also be a recalled unit someone is quietly offloading. Before buying anything secondhand:

- Search the model number on cpsc.gov/recalls.
- Check SaferProducts.gov for incident patterns.
- For tech, our [refurbished tech guide](/posts/best-refurbished-tech-to-buy-2026/) covers how reputable programs handle this; random sellers do not.

Two minutes of checking is the cheapest insurance in shopping.
