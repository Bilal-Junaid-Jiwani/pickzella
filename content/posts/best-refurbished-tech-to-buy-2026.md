Refurbished tech is the best-kept secret in honest value shopping. A one-generation-old phone, laptop, or tablet from a reputable refurbished program does 95% of what the new model does at 60 to 70% of the price, and it arrives tested, cleaned, and warranted. The catch is that "refurbished" is not a regulated word. Anyone can type it. This guide is about buying the seller, not just the device.

## What "refurbished" actually means

Terms first, because sellers blur them on purpose:

- **New:** never owned, full manufacturer warranty.
- **Refurbished / renewed:** previously owned or returned, then inspected, cleaned, repaired if needed, tested, and repackaged. Usually sold with a warranty and return window. The exact standards depend on the program.
- **Open-box:** returned unused or barely used. Often indistinguishable from new, sometimes discounted for the opened packaging.
- **Used:** previously owned, sold as-is. No inspection promised, usually no warranty.

The label matters less than the terms behind it. A "renewed" device with a 90-day guarantee beats a "certified refurbished" device with no stated warranty every time.

## The programs we trust

We recommend programs, not products, because refurbished stock rotates constantly. These are the real, long-running programs with published terms:

**Apple Certified Refurbished.** Apple's own store for returned and repaired devices. Terms as published in 2026: 1-year limited warranty (the same as new), 14-day return window, new battery and outer shell on iOS devices, and AppleCare+ eligibility. Discounts are typically modest, around 15% off new, but the coverage is the best in the business.

**Amazon Renewed.** Amazon's marketplace program for pre-owned, refurbished, and open-box products from approved sellers. Every item carries the Amazon Renewed Guarantee: return it within 90 days of receipt if it does not work as expected. Renewed Premium items extend that to 365 days. Batteries must test at 80% or more of original capacity (90% for Premium), and listings must state the cosmetic condition.

**Manufacturer refurbished stores.** Samsung, Dell, Lenovo, and others run their own refurbished outlets with manufacturer warranties. Worth checking when you have a specific brand in mind; the terms are usually close to Apple's, with smaller discounts.

**Back Market and eBay Refurbished.** Two large marketplaces built specifically for refurbished tech. Both publish seller standards, warranties, and return windows. They are legitimate options, but terms vary by seller tier, so read the specific listing's coverage rather than the marketplace's headline promise.

## What to check before you pay

Run every listing through these three checks:

### 1. Warranty length

The warranty is the seller's confidence in writing. One year (Apple, most manufacturer programs) is excellent. Ninety days (Amazon Renewed) is solid. Thirty days is the floor for anything you should consider. No stated warranty is a dealbreaker, not a discount.

### 2. Return window

The return window is your inspection period. Fourteen days is enough to discover a dud battery or a misdescribed cosmetic grade. Ninety days is generous. If the seller offers no returns, you are not buying refurbished; you are gambling.

### 3. Battery health policy

Batteries are the part that ages even in a drawer. Before buying any refurbished phone, laptop, or tablet, find the battery policy: does the seller fit a new battery (Apple does for iOS devices), guarantee a minimum health percentage (Amazon Renewed: 80%, 90% for Premium), or state nothing at all? "Nothing at all" is the most common answer and the worst one.

## Cosmetic grades, decoded

Most programs grade appearance separately from function. Typical tiers, in plain language:

- **Excellent / Like New:** no visible wear at normal viewing distance. Closest to new, smallest discount.
- **Very Good:** light micro-scratches, invisible in a case. The value sweet spot for most buyers.
- **Good:** visible scratches or small dents. Fine if the device lives in a case or a bag anyway.

Always check whether the grade is the seller's own definition or a program standard, and prefer listings that show actual photos of the device over stock images.

## What to buy refurbished (and what to avoid)

**Great refurbished buys:** phones one or two generations back, business laptops (they were built to be serviced), tablets, smartwatches from manufacturer programs, and game consoles from the console maker's own refurbished store.

**Buy with caution:** wireless earbuds and anything with a tiny sealed battery. Battery degradation hits small devices hardest, and hygiene matters for in-ear gear. If you go this route, stick to manufacturer programs with stated battery and sanitization policies. Our [wireless earbuds guide](/posts/best-wireless-earbuds-under-50-2026/) explains why new budget earbuds are often the better deal here anyway.

**Avoid:** refurbished power banks from unknown sellers (a degraded battery is the whole product), and any "refurbished" listing from a seller with no published warranty, no return window, and no battery policy.

## Refurbished vs. buying new on sale

Do the math before romanticizing refurbished. A new previous-generation phone on a carrier or holiday sale sometimes beats the refurbished price of the same model, with a full warranty and a fresh battery. Refurbished wins biggest on: Apple devices (Apple's discounts are small but the warranty is full), business laptops, and anything where the new model added features you do not need.

## The 5-minute pre-purchase checklist

- Warranty stated in writing, with a length you can live with
- Return window of at least 14 days
- Battery health policy stated (new battery, minimum percentage, or actual figure)
- Cosmetic grade defined, with real photos preferred
- Seller is the manufacturer, an approved program, or a marketplace with a published guarantee
- Price compared against a new previous-generation model on sale

## Final verdict

Refurbished tech from a reputable program is one of the best value moves in tech shopping. Buy the seller first: published warranty, real return window, stated battery policy. Apple Certified Refurbished and Amazon Renewed are the safest defaults, and everything else should be judged against their terms. Check the policy page before you check the price.
